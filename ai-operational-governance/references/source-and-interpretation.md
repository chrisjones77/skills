## What comes from the article

- Pages 1–2: the flight recorder analogy distinguishes retrospective records from
  the work that produces safe operation.
- Pages 2–3: operational compliance produces desired outcomes; procedural compliance
  provides evidence that commitments were kept. The article connects governance,
  programs, systems, and processes and argues that programs and systems are often missing.
- Pages 4–5: management programs assign owners and capabilities, set performance rates,
  and use feedforward and feedback. Technical programs regulate permission, quality,
  qualified operating context, and contributed value. Integrity concerns promises kept.
- Pages 5–6: inventory and audit activity are inadequate proxies for effectiveness;
  the article advocates engineering for governed operation and continuing regulation.

## Interpretation and limits

The skill's explicit control states, latency questions, degraded operation, bypass checks,
measurement denominators, independent recorder/interface design, reviewed feedback, and
worked example are practical elaborations, not quotations
or a complete method supplied by Laqua. Validate them against the actual workflow.

The article invokes the Conant–Ashby theorem and proposes a digital twin connected by
digital threads. This skill does not rely on the theorem to prove safety or require that
architecture. It treats a governance model and traceable connections as design options.
No formal theorem, certification, vendor claim, or real-time guarantee is established here.

The article's sharp recorder analogy is useful for identifying missing control action.
Do not generalize it into a claim that all records are retrospective or incapable of
participating in prevention: monitored records can drive timely controls.
