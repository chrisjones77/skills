# Editorial coverage and revisions

Use two maps: sections establish the song's arc; lyric payoffs establish what each memorable line needs the viewer to see. A shot of a musician does not automatically cover a line about a control panel, missing object or transformation.

For each payoff record the cue, intended meaning, shot/asset IDs, required visible state or action, pace, and inspection result. Classify the requirement as `state` (one frame can show it), `sequence` (before/after states are needed), or `animation` (motion itself matters). A still of a hand on a button shows contact, not a proven press; show a second state or animate the action when the payoff depends on it.

Choose pace locally: sustained for emotional space, conversational for line-by-line exchanges, montage for accumulation, and brief rapid bursts for dense enumerations or musical accents. These are editorial choices, not universal duration thresholds. Hold text and visual gags long enough to read at output size. Leave quiet verses and bridges quiet unless this song calls for escalation. Avoid repeated black fades on short rapid shots; use clean cuts.

When asked for more shots, first find missing payoffs, then add distinct images or action states. Reframing an existing still can serve the edit but does not add unique visual coverage. Report unique source assets, timeline segments and meaningful visual changes separately; document the counting rule for the latter. Never equate a higher cut count with more story.

For timing-preserving revisions, keep an immutable baseline manifest and record its hash, recording hash, section/cut boundaries and protected intervals. New cuts may subdivide approved windows without moving their edges. Verify duration and audio identity as well as boundary inclusion. For protected footage, compare decoded frame hashes at matching frame indices; a matching still-image filename does not establish identical motion, overlays or transitions. State whether audio comparison is source-file identity, encoded stream payload or decoded PCM. Do not claim identical pixels across a new lossy encode without checking them.

For each delivery inspect the actual output geometry, subject crop and text readability. A portrait contact sheet may contain landscape frames, but its layout must never determine production asset dimensions. A letterboxed preview is an explicit exception, not the default production route.
