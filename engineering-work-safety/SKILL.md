# Engineering Work, Safety, and the 0xF Modes

Use this framework to discuss engineering work across **silicon, flesh, and in between**: technology and automation; people affected by the work; and the interfaces, procedures, handoffs, monitoring, management decisions, and organizational conditions that connect them.

**Every worker deserves a safe workplace.** Treat psychosocial safety as a baseline engineering commitment whether or not the 0xF reflection tool is used. Responsibility rests with those who design, deploy, manage, and organize the work; workers should not have to raise concerns or prove harm to merit protection.

## Apply the framework

1. Orient the work: clarify purpose, context, desired outcome, constraints, timing expectations, and affected people and systems.
2. Use 0x0 to orient the work; use a 0x1–0xF code to identify its task class. For work to be dispatched, define a concrete task and its command word; the code classifies the work, while the command says what action to perform.
3. Apply real-time systems discipline to scheduling software work: state relevant releases, priorities, deadlines, effort or execution budgets, dependencies, resource constraints, admission decisions, and overload behavior. Use only the level of timing analysis supported by the task model and evidence.
4. Consider psychosocial effects throughout: workload and pace, role clarity and control, communication and change, support and resources, fairness and respect, worker voice, and barriers to participation.
5. Turn material concerns into an action, owner, interim protection, escalation route, effectiveness check, and revisit point. Keep unresolved material risks visible.
6. Review whether the prompts improved understanding or decisions; simplify or set the tool aside when it adds ceremony without value.

Read [references/framework.md](references/framework.md) for the task-class and command map, task model and scheduling prompts, concern-response path, and lightweight records.

When a named standard informs the discussion, record its identifier, title, edition, and the topic it informs. State whether it is used as inspiration or under a separately established conformity obligation. If applicability is unresolved, mark it as a candidate for review; do not assume it is mandatory or optional. Use the [standards lens record](references/framework.md#standards-lens-record) or reference an existing equivalent record. Keep clause-level evidence and compliance decisions in the separate assessment process.

## Safety commitments

When delivery pressure conflicts with a credible safety concern, make space to assess and address it before proceeding as planned. Schedule, cost, and convenience alone do not justify leaving a material risk unexamined. Unresolved material risks need an authorized decision owner, documented rationale and impacts, interim protections, and a review point. If no owner can resolve the risk, escalate it and keep it visible. These are aspirational framework commitments; they do not establish that an organization has fulfilled them.

When someone reports immediate danger, direct them to applicable emergency, incident, or stop-work channels and appropriate interim protection; do not make them wait for this framework. Receive concerns respectfully, avoid retaliation, limit sensitive records, explain access and confidentiality limits, and use an alternate or independent reporting route when the usual route is implicated. If no safe route exists, surface that process gap to an appropriate higher-level or independent function.

## Boundaries

This framework provides a task vocabulary and a way to apply real-time systems discipline to software-work planning and dispatch. It is not itself a scheduler, timing analysis, or evidence that a workplace is safe or a risk is controlled. It does not replace formal hazard assessment, incident/reporting procedures, required consultation, applicable requirements, or system-specific engineering assurance. It does not establish compliance, a safety case, schedulability, bounded response, deadline compliance, fault tolerance, or safe degraded operation. Do not infer these from mode codes, command words, or completed prompts.

For a formal compliance obligation, identify the applicable requirement and scope, decision authority, reviewer and method, evidence, findings, and follow-up separately. For organizational change, define when consultation applies, who participates, how participation is made safe and accessible, how input informs decisions, and how outcomes are communicated.
