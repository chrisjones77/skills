# Creative Seed Development

Use this skill to turn a supplied premise into a coherent, medium-agnostic creative seed or adapt one across prose, screen, games, visual development, and other formats. It is especially useful when someone wants a familiar archetype or dramatic function without naming its source. This workflow helps develop distinct creative expression; it cannot guarantee non-infringement, ownership, or legal clearance.

## 1. Identify the intended effect

Extract the high-level experience the requester wants: tone, dramatic function, audience, stakes, and intended media. Ask what the requested replacement needs to do in the story, not which signature traits of a known character or faction should be retained. Separate those goals from supplied names, dialogue, visual details, and other concrete expression.

## 2. Make an originality pass

Review the premise as a whole, including combinations of:

- Names, titles, slogans, and invented terminology.
- Character appearance, costume, behavior, and relationships.
- Distinctive vehicles, props, weapons, and silhouettes.
- Setting, scene structure, dialogue, and narrative beats.

Do not assume that changing labels makes a close adaptation original. If the request is to replace a recognizable character, faction, or setting while preserving its signature look, behavior, equipment, and story role, treat that as a close-substitution request. Do not present a renamed bundle of those traits as an original or legally safe design. Instead, retain only the broad narrative function and rebuild the character or faction from different causes, values, institutions, visual logic, technology, tactics, relationships, and dramatic choices. Avoid imitating a named franchise or identifiable creator's style. If the requester asks for legal clearance, provide only a bounded editorial risk assessment and leave legal conclusions to qualified counsel.

## 3. Build the seed from independent choices

Write a compact seed containing:

1. A one-sentence premise and the intended tone.
2. A distinct setting and conflict.
3. Character motivations, choices, and limitations.
4. A coherent visual or sensory language.
5. Technology or props whose functions follow from the setting.
6. A short sample scene or interaction that demonstrates the voice without leaning on familiar lines or scene patterns.

Prefer a few connected, specific decisions over a checklist of familiar genre markers. Make the internal logic explain why the characters and systems behave as they do. For a replacement concept, ensure its defining traits arise from the new world's own history and constraints rather than serving as one-to-one aliases for a known property.

## 4. Adapt for each medium

- **Visual development:** Describe composition, materials, lighting, silhouette, and mood using the seed's own design language. Resolve terms such as 'gems' or other creative metaphors from the conceptual context before literal image generation to avoid incorrect literal interpretations. Avoid embedding claims of legal safety in image prompts.
- **Games:** Convert motivations into observable goals, constraints, and player-facing behaviors. Tie mechanics to the setting and explain counterplay and variation.
- **Screen and prose:** Use voice, action, pacing, and consequences to express character. Write new dialogue rather than paraphrasing recognizable quotations.
- **3D and production design:** Describe function, scale, materials, assembly, and distinctive shape language; identify what is fixed and what may vary across iterations.

Keep a short canon sheet so names, motives, technology, and tone remain consistent when moving between formats. Record the origin of supplied material and distinguish it from newly invented details when provenance matters.

## 5. Audit a catalogue concern and manage corrective action

Use this workflow when the requester asks to assess a concern across a defined body of creative work. Treat it as an ISO-informed, evidence-based review adapted to creative material. ISO 19011 provides guidance for management-system audits; this skill does not perform an ISO audit, assess conformity to an ISO standard, provide legal clearance, or certify a catalogue. Use the current edition and applicable ISO material when the user requests standards-specific work.

1. **Define the review.** Record the concern, intended decision, scope and exclusions, review criteria, item population, time/version, reviewer competence or limitations, confidentiality needs, and any conflicts that could affect independence. Define terms such as “provenance concern,” “similarity concern,” and “public-release risk.” A concern is not automatically a confirmed finding or a standards nonconformity.
2. **Establish coverage.** Build an inventory with stable item identifiers and versions. For full item-level coverage against the stated criteria, examine every in-scope item and relevant associated material (for example, titles, lyrics, descriptions, credits, artwork, prompts, and cross-item references). If using sampling, document the method, rationale, population, exclusions, and limits; do not generalize sampled results to unreviewed items without support.
3. **Gather and evaluate evidence.** Use relevant, authorized source records and review criteria. Record each observation with its item locator, evidence, criterion, reasoning, confidence, and unresolved questions. Keep direct evidence, interpretation, and speculation distinct. Protect private source material and avoid copying unnecessary sensitive or copyrighted content into a broad report.
4. **Report findings.** Produce a dated report stating objective, scope, criteria, method and coverage, evidence basis, confirmed findings, concerns needing more evidence, limitations, risks, and recommendations. Give findings unique IDs and calibrated priority with rationale. Include a clear executive summary and an item-level appendix or restricted evidence register as appropriate. Do not claim universal clearance from a limited review.
5. **Plan correction and corrective action.** For each accepted finding, assign an accountable owner, interim containment or release control where needed, specific correction, cause analysis proportionate to the evidence, check for similar cases elsewhere in the corpus, preventive/system action, due date, and objective completion evidence. Distinguish correction of an individual item from corrective action intended to prevent recurrence. Preserve provenance and the audit trail in an appropriately controlled record; public-facing remediation may revise or withhold material, but should not falsify authorship, evidence, or history.
6. **Verify and close.** An appropriate reviewer checks the change against the stated criteria, verifies evidence, and evaluates whether the action worked across the relevant scope. Record residual risk, accepted exceptions, closure authority, and any follow-up date. Reopen or extend the action when evidence shows recurrence or ineffective controls. Keep unresolved items visibly unresolved.

This is a practical review and improvement pattern informed by audit and corrective-action concepts; it is not a substitute for the full standards, an independent audit engagement, or legal advice.

## 6. Deliver with accurate status

Present creative seeds as drafts, not legal certificates. State meaningful assumptions or unresolved originality questions plainly. Do not claim that an unreviewed asset, prompt, or finished work is cleared for use. Keep private source material out of public or shared outputs unless the requester authorizes its inclusion.
