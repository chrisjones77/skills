# Advisory summary and source boundary

## Source

- **Document:** Engineers and Geoscientists BC, *Practice Advisory: Use of Artificial Intelligence (AI) in Professional Practice*
- **Version:** 1.0
- **Published:** November 22, 2024
- **Scope:** Professional registrants using or interacting with third-party AI systems and tools in engineering or geoscience work. It does not address designing or developing AI systems.
- **Ontario cover note in supplied PDF:** Professional Engineers Ontario adopted the EGBC document as guidance for Ontario. Ontario practitioners are directed to use applicable Ontario statutes, regulations, PEO's Code of Ethics, and other applicable employer or project policies instead of BC-specific law and regulation.

The supplied PDF includes a one-page PEO cover note followed by the ten-page EGBC advisory. Confirm that later regulator guidance has not superseded it when current compliance matters.

## Controlling principles

1. The professional remains responsible for work generated by or incorporating AI output.
2. Public safety, health, welfare, environmental protection, and workplace safety remain paramount.
3. Use AI only within the professional's competence, including competence to identify and mitigate AI-related risks.
4. Conduct and document a risk assessment for professional activities or work as required by the governing regime.
5. Meet quality-management requirements throughout the work: direct supervision, documented checks, project-document retention, and independent review for high-risk work.
6. Understand the specific tool, intended function, limitations, data, and changing behaviour. Vendor validation alone is not enough for a specific professional application.
7. Apply professional and ethical judgment continuously; AI use is not a one-time procurement decision.

## AI categories used by the advisory

- **Static AI:** Behaviour is locked after training. Identical inputs are expected to produce identical outputs, although small input changes can still cause unexpectedly large output changes.
- **Dynamic AI:** Adapts, learns, or evolves. Outputs may change across uses or over time, creating additional repeatability, validation, monitoring, and records concerns.

## Risk factors identified

- computational/statistical and human-cognitive bias;
- trustworthiness, including validity, reliability, safety, security, resilience, accountability, transparency, explainability, interpretability, privacy enhancement, and fairness;
- lack of transparency, explainability, or interpretability;
- lack of repeatability, including version-driven changes;
- confidentiality and privacy loss through third-party access or model training;
- hallucinated, inaccurate, or nonsensical output;
- human overdependence and erosion of critical thinking;
- privacy, copyright, trademark, intellectual-property, and ownership issues;
- environmental, equity, fairness, and inclusivity impacts.

## Risk questions emphasized

- Is AI supporting fully verifiable work, or making/affecting decisions that cannot be fully checked?
- What happens if the output is wrong?
- Can the professional accept full responsibility for the resulting work?
- Is the system dynamic or otherwise non-repeatable?
- What training data and biases may influence the output?
- Who owns or may use the input and output data?
- Is the user competent in both the professional domain and the AI use?
- Has the provider validated the system, and is evidence available?
- Can the system be validated for this exact application? By whom, how often, and against what acceptance criteria?

## Quality-management implications

### Direct supervision

A professional supervising delegated work containing AI use must remain aware of and actively involved in the work and apply the same standard of care as if using the tool directly.

### Documented checks

Controls may include:

- recording the AI system make and version;
- testing a range of cases;
- recording validation inputs and outputs;
- comparing results with known calculations or an independent method;
- recording who validated the system, when, and what corrective action was required;
- validating dynamic AI output on each use when risk warrants it.

### Project documentation

Incidental grammar or structural assistance that is fully reviewed may require little or no special documentation. AI-generated results or design decisions require records comparable to those kept if the professional had performed the research or calculations directly.

The BC advisory states a minimum retention period of 10 years after project completion, or 10 years after a continuing-use document is retired. Verify the applicable rule outside BC and any stricter contract, insurer, employer, or project requirement.

### High-risk work

Where the applicable risk assessment classifies professional work as high risk, obtain an independent review by a separate qualified professional under the governing quality-management requirements.

## Firm and disclosure considerations

Firms should maintain AI governance policies, understand product terms and intended operation, keep knowledge current, and train personnel. ISO/IEC 42001:2023 is identified as a potentially useful AI management-system standard.

The advisory does not set a blanket AI disclosure requirement. It does require documented risk assessment where applicable and recommends considering communication of AI-related risks to the client. Contracts, legislation, regulator rules, ethics, and good-faith obligations may independently require disclosure.

## Limits

This summary is a working aid, not legal advice and not a verbatim reproduction of the advisory. Use the original document and current governing sources for formal compliance decisions.
