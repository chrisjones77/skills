# EGBC AI Professional Practice

Apply the advisory as a professional-practice control framework. Treat AI as a tool: the responsible professional retains full responsibility for work generated by or incorporating AI output.

## Start with the source boundary

Read [references/advisory-summary.md](references/advisory-summary.md) before giving a substantive assessment.

- Identify the governing jurisdiction, regulator, contract, employer policy, and project quality-management system.
- Treat the advisory as guidance, not a substitute for current legislation, bylaws, codes of ethics, standards, legal advice, or project requirements.
- For Ontario work, apply the PEO cover note: use Ontario statutes, regulations, PEO's Code of Ethics, and other applicable policies instead of BC-specific provisions.
- State when a current legal, regulatory, contractual, licensing, privacy, or intellectual-property question requires verification or qualified advice.

## Apply the assessment workflow

### 1. Define the proposed AI use

Record:

- professional activity, deliverable, lifecycle stage, and intended user;
- AI product, provider, model/version, configuration, and whether it is static or dynamic;
- input data, output, integration points, and human decision points;
- whether AI merely assists work that can be fully checked or performs/affects a decision that cannot be fully verified before use;
- potential effects on public safety, health, welfare, property, economic interests, the environment, and workplace safety.

Do not assess "AI" in the abstract. Assess the specific tool, version, data, use, and consequence pathway.

### 2. Apply the competence gate

Determine whether the responsible professional is competent to:

- understand the professional domain and intended AI function;
- identify limitations, failure modes, and use-specific risks;
- interpret and challenge the output;
- reproduce or independently substantiate the result using conventional methods where necessary;
- design and evaluate suitable validation.

If these conditions are not met, recommend not using the tool for that activity unless appropriately qualified expertise and controls close the gap. Do not present training, vendor claims, or fluent output as evidence of professional competence.

### 3. Complete a documented risk assessment

Use [references/assessment-framework.md](references/assessment-framework.md) and copy [assets/ai-use-risk-assessment-template.md](assets/ai-use-risk-assessment-template.md) into the user's project when a formal record is needed.

For each hazard, document:

- cause or failure mode;
- affected people, assets, obligations, or environment;
- consequence and severity;
- likelihood and uncertainty;
- initial risk;
- preventive, detective, and corrective controls;
- residual risk, owner, evidence, and review frequency.

Always consider bias, unjustified trust, lack of transparency/explainability/interpretability, non-repeatability, confidentiality/privacy, hallucination or factual error, overdependence, intellectual-property rights, security/resilience, model or version changes, environmental effects, and equity/fairness/inclusivity.

### 4. Design verification and quality controls

Match controls to the risk and the role of the AI:

- Validate against known cases, hand calculations, authoritative sources, past confirmed work, or an independent method.
- Use a representative range of normal, boundary, adverse, and out-of-distribution test cases.
- Record inputs, outputs, expected results, deviations, reviewer, date, tool/model/version, and corrective actions.
- For dynamic AI or changing hosted services, consider validation on every use and after any model, configuration, data, or provider change.
- Require human review by a competent person before professional reliance.
- Apply direct-supervision requirements to delegated work containing AI use as rigorously as if the supervising professional used the tool directly.
- Require a separate qualified professional to conduct an independent review when the work is classified as high risk under the applicable regime.

Copy [assets/ai-validation-log-template.md](assets/ai-validation-log-template.md) when traceable test evidence is needed.

### 5. Address information and rights

Before entering project information into a third-party system, determine:

- whether the information is confidential, personal, privileged, export-controlled, security-sensitive, or owned by another party;
- provider access, retention, training use, data location, deletion, and breach terms;
- ownership and permitted use of inputs and outputs;
- contractual, client, insurer, employer, licensing, and disclosure restrictions.

Do not recommend uploading protected information unless the authorization and safeguards are established. Escalate unresolved privacy, confidentiality, licensing, or intellectual-property questions.

### 6. Define records, disclosure, and monitoring

Document material AI use to the same defensible standard as the research, calculations, or decisions it assisted or replaced. Record at least:

- purpose and scope of use;
- tool/model/version and relevant settings;
- material inputs, outputs, prompts or configuration where needed for traceability;
- risk assessment and approvals;
- validation and checking evidence;
- decisions, deviations, corrections, and responsible reviewers;
- independent review, disclosure, and monitoring requirements.

Use [assets/ai-use-project-record-template.md](assets/ai-use-project-record-template.md) for the project file.

The advisory states a BC retention period of at least 10 years after project end, or after a document used in continuing work is no longer in use. Verify and apply the governing jurisdictional, contractual, and organizational retention rule; do not blindly transpose the BC period.

Do not claim that disclosure is never required. Check contracts, law, regulator guidance, client expectations, and material risk. Recommend disclosure when required or when fairness, good faith, informed decision-making, or risk communication calls for it.

## Produce a decision-ready result

Structure assessments as:

1. **Determination:** acceptable, acceptable with conditions, unsuitable pending controls, or unsuitable for the proposed use.
2. **Basis:** jurisdiction, activity, AI role, competence, consequence, and ability to verify.
3. **Risk register:** hazards, ratings, controls, residual risk, owners, and evidence.
4. **Validation and checking plan:** test method, acceptance criteria, frequency, reviewer, and records.
5. **Quality-management requirements:** supervision, documentation, retention, and independent review.
6. **Information governance:** confidentiality, privacy, security, intellectual property, and provider terms.
7. **Disclosure and monitoring:** who must be informed, what changes trigger reassessment, and review dates.
8. **Open issues:** facts or professional advice still required.

Use precise conditional language. Distinguish advisory guidance from mandatory obligations and identify assumptions rather than inventing missing project facts.
